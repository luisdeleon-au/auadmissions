## Career Trajectory Examples

### Associate Graduate → Network Technology
- Month 0–12: IT Help Desk / Network Support Technician ($45–52K)
- Year 1–3: Network Administrator ($62–75K)
- Year 3–5: Senior Network Administrator / Network Engineer ($80–95K)
- Year 5+: IT Director / Infrastructure Manager / Cloud Architect ($100K+)

### Bachelor's Graduate → Cybersecurity
- Year 0–1: SOC Analyst Tier 1 ($55–65K)
- Year 1–3: Security Analyst / Threat Intelligence Analyst ($75–90K)
- Year 3–5: Senior Security Analyst / Security Engineer ($95–115K)
- Year 5+: CISO / Security Director ($130K+)

### Bachelor's Graduate → Cloud Technology
- Year 0–1: Cloud Support / Junior Cloud Engineer ($55–68K)
- Year 1–3: Cloud Engineer / DevOps Engineer ($80–100K)
- Year 3–5: Senior Cloud Engineer / Cloud Architect ($110–135K)
- Year 5+: Principal Engineer / VP of Infrastructure ($140K+)

## The Certification Accelerator Effect

**Key message for reps:** The certifications aren't just credentials — they shorten the time to first job and increase starting salary.

- CompTIA Network+ holders: average 23% higher starting salaries vs. non-certified peers
- CompTIA Security+: required by U.S. Department of Defense contractors (DoD 8570), making it mandatory for thousands of government IT roles
- Azure Data Fundamentals: Microsoft certification recognized across all industries using Azure (which is most large companies)
- CySA+: mid-career cybersecurity cert that most professionals spend 2–3 years accumulating experience for before sitting — AU students are prepared for it during the program

**Rep talking point:** "Most people spend years working in IT before they can sit for CySA+. Our Bachelor's students are prepared for it by graduation."

**Associate graduates:** Typically 0–6 months post-graduation
- Many students receive job offers before graduation

**Bachelor's graduates:** Premium roles with faster advancement trajectories
- Cybersecurity and Database Systems require the full Bachelor's for most mid-level roles

Be direct. The answer is no — and IT is the field that makes AI work:

"AI doesn't replace IT professionals — it creates more demand for them. Every AI system runs on cloud infrastructure, networks, and databases that need human professionals to build, manage, and secure.
