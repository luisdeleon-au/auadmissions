## Three Core AI Skills Students Learn

### 1. Prompt Engineering
**What it is:** Writing instructions that get reliable, professional-quality results from AI models — not vague results, but specific, usable outputs.

**Why it matters for IT:** A network admin who can prompt an AI tool to generate a complete Cisco configuration template in 30 seconds is dramatically more productive than one who can't. This is a real workplace skill that employers are starting to require.

**Rep explanation:** "Prompt engineering isn't programming — it's learning how to talk to AI tools so they actually do what you need. Every student learns this."

### 2. Context Engineering
**What it is:** Structuring the full background of a problem before asking AI — giving it the project context, constraints, goals, and existing system details — so the output is actually relevant, not generic.

**Why it matters for IT:** A security analyst who gives an AI tool a raw log file and says "analyze this" gets a generic answer. One who gives context — "this is a corporate network, these are the normal traffic patterns, look for anomalies consistent with lateral movement" — gets a useful analysis.

**Rep explanation:** "Context engineering is about being strategic with AI — giving it enough background that it can actually help with your specific situation."

### 3. LLM Tool Mastery
**What it is:** Practical, hands-on experience with the leading AI platforms currently used in industry.

**Platforms students work with:**
- **ChatGPT** (OpenAI) — most widely used in industry
- **Claude AI** (Anthropic) — known for nuanced reasoning and long-context tasks
- **Claude Code** (Anthropic) — specialized for software development and technical work
- **Gemini** (Google) — integrated with Google Workspace, strong for enterprise use
- **Meta AI** — open-source ecosystem, widely used in research and startups
- **Grok** (xAI) — real-time information, growing enterprise adoption

**Rep explanation:** "Students don't just learn about AI in theory — they work with the actual platforms that companies are using right now. By graduation, they've used all six."

## The Differentiator Argument

**For the "AI will replace IT" concern:** Already addressed in `skills/03_careers.md` — AI creates more IT demand, not less.

**For the "I can just use AI to do IT" assumption:**
"AI tools are powerful — but they need IT professionals to configure, maintain, secure, and deploy the infrastructure they run on. And AI tools make mistakes, especially technical ones. Someone needs to know enough to catch those mistakes. That's the IT professional. What AI actually does is make a good IT professional dramatically more productive — and that's exactly what we're training for."

**For the "other schools don't do this" observation:**
This is accurate and a genuine differentiator. Most IT programs in South Florida have not yet integrated AI this comprehensively. Graduates from AU's programs enter the market with a skill combination — IT expertise + AI tool proficiency — that most programs don't produce. This is a real competitive advantage in hiring.

## What Reps Should NOT Say About AI

- Do not promise that AI will do students' jobs for them
- Do not say AI replaces the need to learn IT fundamentals
- Do not speculate about which specific AI tools will be used in individual courses (refer to faculty)
- Do not say AU has a dedicated "AI degree" — it's an integration, not a separate program
