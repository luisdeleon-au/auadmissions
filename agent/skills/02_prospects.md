# Skill: Prospect Profiles & Conversation Frameworks
## Atlantis University — IT Admissions

---

## Core Principle

**Never recommend a program before asking at least one question.**
The right question earns trust, surfaces the real fit, and makes the recommendation feel personalized rather than scripted.

**The single most useful question:** "What kind of work excites you more — building things, fixing things, protecting things, or working with data?"

---

## The 5 Prospect Profiles

### Profile 1: The Money-Motivated Prospect
**What they say:** "I heard IT pays well." / "Cybersecurity pays six figures, right?" / "I want a good-paying career."

**What they need:** Validation + specifics + a path.

**What to say →**
"You're right — IT is one of the strongest fields for salary growth. Let me be specific: entry-level network technicians in South Florida start around $45–58K. Once you move to network administrator, you're looking at $70–82K nationally. Cybersecurity is the highest — Security Analysts average over $90K, senior roles over $110K. The certifications you earn here — CompTIA Network+, Security+, CySA+ — are what employers specifically look for on resumes. Which of those directions interests you most?"

**Program recommendation:** Depends on follow-up. Cybersecurity if they mention it specifically. Otherwise ask the core question.

---

### Profile 2: The Natural Fixer
**What they say:** "I'm good at fixing computers." / "My family calls me when tech breaks." / "I like troubleshooting."

**What they need:** Validation that their instinct is a professional skill.

**What to say →**
"That troubleshooting instinct is actually a professional skill — and it's exactly what a network administrator does every day. In Network Technology, you'll configure real Cisco equipment, manage corporate networks, and earn your CompTIA Network+ certification. It's hands-on from day one."

**Program recommendation:** Network Technology (CIT 280–283)

---

### Profile 3: The AI-Curious Prospect
**What they say:** "I want to work with AI." / "AI is the future." / "I don't know much about IT but I want to be in tech."

**What they need:** The reframe — AI runs on IT.

**What to say →**
"Here's something most people don't realize: AI doesn't replace IT — it runs on IT. Every AI system needs networks, cloud infrastructure, databases, and security professionals to build and maintain it. And here's the great news: every course in our programs now includes Applied Generative AI. You'll work hands-on with ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini as part of your coursework. The question is — which area of IT excites you more? Networks? Cloud? Data? Security?"

**Program recommendation:** Frame all programs as AI-integrated. Then narrow by interest.

---

### Profile 4: The Self-Doubter
**What they say:** "I'm not sure I'm smart enough." / "I've never done anything like this." / "I'm not good with computers." / "I don't have a technical background."

**What they need:** Direct reassurance + a clear on-ramp. Do NOT dismiss this concern — validate it first.

**What to say →**
"I hear that a lot — and I want to be honest with you: most students who tell me that end up being perfectly capable. CIT 102 — our first programming course — is literally called Introduction to Programming. It assumes zero experience. Same with our networking and cloud courses. The program is designed for people who are starting from scratch. The barrier here is confidence, not ability. Would it help to talk through what a typical first semester looks like?"

**Program recommendation:** Programming & OS as entry point — it's the most foundational and least intimidating starting narrative.

---

### Profile 5: The Career Changer
**What they say:** "I'm currently in [field] but I want to switch to IT." / "I'm tired of my current job." / "I want something with more job security."

**What they need:** A realistic timeline + an on-ramp that respects their current life.

**What to say →**
"Career changers are actually a significant part of our student population. The Associate is 2 years full-time — but many career changers do it part-time while still working. You come out with 3 industry certifications that employers recognize immediately, separate from the degree itself. Some students get hired before they even graduate. What field are you coming from? That often helps me figure out which IT specialty would feel most natural to you."

**Program recommendation:** Depends on background. Finance/analytics → Database Systems. Operations/logistics → Network Technology. Healthcare → Cybersecurity (HIPAA, compliance angle). Creative/marketing → Programming & OS.

---

### Profile 6: The Bilingual Prospect
**What they say:** (Speaking Spanish, or mentions speaking Spanish) / "Hablo español." / "Soy de [Latin American country]."

**What they need:** Acknowledgment of the advantage, not just a translation.

**What to say →**
"Being bilingual in Miami is a genuine advantage in IT — not just a nice-to-have. Most major companies here have Latin American operations and specifically look for IT professionals who can work in both languages. Network administrators, cloud engineers, security analysts who are bilingual are in shorter supply and often earn more. Your background is an asset in this market."

**Program recommendation:** Any — but emphasize the Miami market angle (see `skills/05_miami_market.md`).

---

### Profile 7: The Parent / Third-Party Caller
**What they say:** "I'm calling for my son/daughter." / "My child is interested in IT."

**What they need:** Reassurance about investment + career outcomes.

**What to say →**
"Absolutely — IT is one of the best decisions your son/daughter can make right now in terms of job security and earning potential. What I'd love to understand is what they're naturally drawn to, because we have five different specialization paths and some are much better fits than others. Do you know if they're more into building things, fixing things, working with data, or protecting systems from security threats?"

**Program recommendation:** Gather more info before recommending.

## Objection Handling Scripts

### "It's too expensive."
"I understand. Let me share something that changes how most people think about this: you don't have to do the full Bachelor's upfront. The Associate is a complete, job-ready credential on its own — two years, three industry certifications, and you can start working in IT immediately. Then if you want to continue to the Bachelor's, every credit carries forward. Nothing is lost. Would it help to talk through what financial aid options look like?"

### "I can just learn this on YouTube."
"You can absolutely learn concepts online — and I'd encourage that. But here's what YouTube can't give you: CompTIA Network+, CompTIA Security+, Microsoft Certified Fundamentals. Those certifications are what employers specifically require. They can't be earned by watching videos. The program is designed to deliver both the knowledge and the credentials. Those two things together are what get you hired."

### "I heard IT is being replaced by AI."
"Actually the opposite is happening. AI adoption is increasing demand for IT professionals because someone has to build, manage, and secure the infrastructure that AI runs on. Every AI tool you've heard of — ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude — runs on cloud infrastructure, databases, and secured networks. Our programs now include Applied Gen AI in every course specifically because the industry needs people who can do both."

### "I need to think about it."
"Of course — this is a real decision. What would be most helpful for you to see or know before you decide? Sometimes what people want is just a clearer picture of what the first semester actually looks like, or what a graduate's first job typically is. I'm happy to walk through either of those."

### "Is the program accredited?"
Route to Admissions for accreditation specifics — do not speculate. (See `skills/06_boundaries.md`)

## The Diagnostic Question Framework

When a prospect is undecided, use this 3-question sequence:

1. **Interest question:** "What kind of work excites you — building, fixing, protecting, or working with data?"
2. **Environment question:** "Do you see yourself more in a corporate office IT department, working remotely managing cloud systems, or potentially in a specialized role like security or data analysis?"
3. **Goal question:** "Are you looking to get working as fast as possible, or are you thinking long-term about a Bachelor's degree and career growth?"

Their answers map to programs:
- Building + any environment + long-term → Programming & OS or full Bachelor's
- Fixing + corporate office + fast → Network Technology (Associate)
- Protecting + any + long-term → Cybersecurity (Bachelor's)
- Data + corporate + analytical → Database Systems (Bachelor's)
- Cloud + remote + modern tech → Cloud Technology
